Frequently asked questions

My EOB doesn't match my bill — is that normal?

It's common, and it doesn't automatically mean you owe the higher number. Your insurer sends the EOB and the provider sends the bill separately, often on different timelines, and it's easy for the two to be out of sync — because of a processing delay, a coding issue, or a genuine billing error. The fix isn't to guess; it's to put both documents side by side, line by line, and see exactly where they diverge before you pay or dispute anything.

What's the difference between a medical bill and an EOB?

The bill comes from your provider and shows what they're asking you to pay. The EOB (Explanation of Benefits) comes from your insurance company and shows how they processed the claim — the billed amount, what your plan's contract allows, what insurance paid, and what they say you actually owe. The EOB is not a bill itself; it's the record of what your insurer decided. When your patient responsibility on the EOB is lower than what the bill is asking for, that gap is exactly what this tool flags.

The hospital didn't give me an itemized bill — what do I do?

Ask for one before you do anything else. A summary bill just shows a total; an itemized bill breaks that total down by every service, code, and charge, which is what you actually need to compare against your EOB line by line. You're entitled to request it — call the billing office directly and ask for an itemized statement, not just a balance due.

What if this was a surprise bill, an ER visit, or an out-of-network provider at an in-network facility?

If you were treated in an emergency room, or by an out-of-network provider at a facility that's otherwise in-network, the federal No Surprises Act may limit what you can be balance-billed for. It doesn't cover every situation, but it's worth knowing before you pay — the No Surprises Help Desk (1-800-985-3059) can tell you whether your specific bill qualifies. Confirm current details directly, since program specifics can change.

What do I actually say when I call to dispute it?

This is the part most tools skip. Knowing there's a mismatch isn't the same as knowing how to get it fixed, which is why the Medical Bill Mismatch Tracker includes a full phone dispute script — not a generic template, a word-for-word guide for the actual call: what to ask the billing supervisor, what documentation to reference, and how to follow up if the first call doesn't resolve it.

How do I know if it's a billing error or something I actually owe?

Common causes of a mismatch include duplicate charges, a service billed at the wrong code, a claim that hasn't fully finished processing yet, or a provider billing before the insurance payment posted. None of these mean you're wrong to question it. The starting rule is simple: if the bill is asking for more than your EOB's stated patient responsibility, that gap is worth a specific question to the billing office, not an assumption that you owe it.

My claim was denied, not just mismatched — can this help?

Not directly, and it's worth being upfront about that rather than overselling it. This tool is built for a bill that doesn't match what your EOB says you owe — a reconciliation problem. A denied claim is a different situation: your insurer decided not to cover the service at all, which involves an appeals process rather than a line-item comparison. If your claim was denied outright, look for insurance-appeal resources built specifically for that process instead.

Does this work for tracking multiple bills, not just one?

Yes — it's built to hold more than one claim at a time, which matters if you're managing an ongoing course of treatment, a hospital stay with several providers billing separately, or bills on behalf of a family member.
